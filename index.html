Welcome to the PDD Unit

This hub is your home base for the internship. Once you're unlocked into your own private space, you'll move through induction and the different functions of the unit, from admin and the LMS through to creative design and course development, all in one place. Everything you need to get oriented, reflect on your progress and check in with your coach lives inside your Intern Space.

Our theoretical basis: the GROW model

Your development through this internship is guided by the GROW coaching model. Each month, in your private space, you'll work through these four steps with your coach to reflect on how far you've come and what's next.

🎯 Goal

What do you want to achieve in this function, this month?

🔍 Reality

Where are you right now, honestly? What can you already do?

💡 Options

What support, people or resources could help you grow?

🚀 Way Forward

What will you actually do before your next check-in?

🧭 Welcome to your Induction Programme

These activities build on each other, so work through them in order. Start with getting to know your fellow interns, then move into Upskilling 101, where you'll build the thinking behind good design, good digital filing, and ten practical Canva skills you'll prove in a design of your own.

Activity 1 · Get to know each other · ±30 min Activity 2 · Design principles · ±60–75 min Activity 3 · Digital filing · ±45–60 min Activity 4 · Canva skills · ±2 hours

🤝 Ground rules for the whole programme

  • You may assist each other throughout, but you may not do the work for each other. Explain, demonstrate on your own screen, point at a menu, then let your fellow intern do it themselves.
  • After every activity, ask a fellow intern: "What's one question that could help me extend or improve my reflective notes, or my design?" Note their question and your answer in your Reflection Journal.
  • Every intern completes their own reflective notes and their own Canva design.

Capture your reflections for each activity in the Reflection Journal tab of your Intern Space, it mirrors these activities question for question.

🎯 The goal

By the end of this week you'll know your fellow interns, the language of design, a clean filing habit, and ten Canva skills, proven in one design.

🎲 Activity 1 · Get to know each other

±30 minutes · pairs, then rotate

How to play: sit in pairs. Take turns drawing a question number (1 to 15) at random, you can't pick your own. Answer honestly; your partner may ask one follow-up. After five questions each, rotate partners and continue. If a question has already been answered for you, draw again.

The questions

  1. What's the story behind your name, or a nickname you've had?
  2. What made you apply for this internship?
  3. What's one thing you're genuinely good at that isn't on your CV?
  4. Describe your ideal working environment: music or silence? Morning or night?
  5. What app, tool, or website could you not live without, and why?
  6. What's a skill you're hoping to walk away from this internship with?
  7. Who is someone, famous or not, whose work or style you admire?
  8. What's the best piece of advice anyone has given you?
  9. If you had a free Saturday and no obligations, how would you spend it?
  10. What's something that recently made you laugh?
  11. Coffee, tea, or something else entirely?
  12. What's one thing people often get wrong about you at first?
  13. When you have to learn something new, do you watch, read, or just try it?
  14. What's a small win from the past month you're proud of?
  15. What do you hope this intern group is like by the end of the programme?

💬 Reflect afterwards

In your Reflection Journal, note: one thing you have in common with another intern, one thing that surprised you, and who you'd go to first for help with a design question, and why.

📚 Upskilling 101

Three activities that build the foundational skills you'll use throughout your internship: how good design works, how to keep your files in order, and ten practical Canva skills you'll prove in a design of your own.

🎨 Activity 2 · Elements & principles of design

±60–75 minutes · work alone, then compare in pairs

Work through the four resources below in order. They cover the building blocks of visual and media design, line, shape, colour, texture, space, and the principles that arrange them: balance, contrast, hierarchy, alignment, repetition, proximity, and unity.

📚 Resources

Video

Fundamentals of Design

A short video introducing the building blocks of design, line, shape, form, texture, and balance, with clear visual examples.

Video

Layout and Composition

Covers the five principles that make a layout work: proximity, white space, alignment, contrast, and repetition.

Video

Use of Colour

An introduction to working with colour, hue, saturation, and value, and how to choose colour combinations that work well together.

Article

The principles of Design

A deeper walkthrough of balance, unity, movement, emphasis, and visual weight, with worked examples of each principle.

✍️ Reflective questions, write these in your journal

  1. In your own words, what is the difference between an element of design and a principle of design? Give one example of each.
  2. Which principle (contrast, alignment, repetition, proximity, balance, hierarchy) do you think matters most for the kind of work our unit produces? Why?
  3. Find one real design around you, a poster, packaging, or a social media post. Which principles is it using well, and which is it breaking?
  4. Which concept from the resources was completely new to you, and how would you explain it to a friend in one sentence?
  5. How do you think you'll apply white space in your own Canva design in Activity 4?

👥 Compare notes with a fellow intern

Swap journals, or read your answers aloud, with one other intern. Where do your definitions differ? Agree together on the three principles you both think are most important, and each add one idea from your partner's notes to your own, credit them by name. Then ask each other: "What one question would extend or improve my notes?"

🗂️ Activity 3 · Data management & digital filing

±45–60 minutes · work alone, then compare in pairs

Messy files cost teams hours. These three resources cover how to name files, structure folders, and manage versions so that anyone on the team can find anything, including you, six months from now.

📚 Resources

Guide

Harvard Medical School — File Naming Conventions

A practical page on building a file naming convention: what to include, what to avoid, dates as YYYY-MM-DD, and documenting your convention in a README file.

Guide

CESSDA Data Management Expert Guide — File Naming and Folder Structure

Best practices and worked examples of useful file names, versioning, and organising folders for a project, with good and bad examples side by side.

Guide

USNH — Naming Conventions & Best Practices for Files and Folders (OneDrive, Teams, SharePoint)

How the same rules apply in shared cloud storage: short descriptive names, underscores or dashes instead of spaces, avoiding special characters and deep folder nesting.

✍️ Reflective questions, write these in your journal

  1. Write out the file naming convention you will use for your internship work, and give one example filename that follows it (e.g. 2026-08-31_ProjectName_Draft_v01.docx).
  2. Sketch (or type) the folder structure you'll use for this internship, top-level folders and one level of subfolders.
  3. Describe a time messy files or lost versions caused you (or someone you know) a problem. Which practice from the resources would have prevented it?
  4. How will you handle versions: what marks a draft, and what marks a final? What does "FINAL_final_v2" tell you about a broken system?
  5. Why do spaces and special characters in filenames cause trouble in shared drives and links?

👥 Compare notes with a fellow intern

Compare your example filenames and folder sketches with a partner. Try to "break" each other's system: name a file the other person's convention can't handle cleanly. Then agree on one shared convention the two of you would recommend for the whole intern group, and write it in both journals. Finish by asking: "What one question would extend or improve my notes?"

🖌️ Activity 4 · Ten basic Canva skills, build a design

±2 hours · individual design, peer assistance allowed

Set up your own Canva account, learn ten basic skills, then prove them all in one design of your own.

🚀 Step A · Set up your Canva account

  1. Go to canva.com and create a free account using your internship email address.
  2. Work through the free Canva Essentials course (about 60 minutes of short videos) to get oriented.
  3. Create a new design, a good starting size is Poster or Instagram Post.

✅ Step B · The ten skills, tick them off as you learn each one

Each skill links to a resource demonstrating it. Watch or read it, then practise the skill inside your own design.

🎯 Step C · Design something that shows all ten skills

Your brief: design a one-page "Meet the intern" poster or social post about yourself (or, if you prefer, a poster for an imaginary team event). Your single design must visibly demonstrate all ten skills: it starts from a customised template, contains formatted text, at least two Elements, one uploaded image, deliberate text-over-image treatment, aligned and grouped items, a precisely cropped photo, and you must resize it into a second format and share it.

While you work, apply what you learned in Activity 2: check your contrast, alignment, hierarchy, and white space. And practise Activity 3: name your downloaded files using your new naming convention.

  1. Build your design, ticking off each skill in the checklist above as you use it.
  2. Click Share → Copy link (set to "anyone with the link can view") and add the link as a new entry in your Reflection Journal, tagged Progress.
  3. Download one copy as PNG or PDF, named according to your Activity 3 convention.

👥 Peer review, assist, don't do

Show your design to at least one fellow intern. You may guide each other ("try Position → Align"), but never touch each other's design. Ask each other these questions and record the answers in your journal:

  1. "What one change would most improve my design, and which design principle does it relate to?"
  2. "Which of the ten skills is least visible in my design, and how could I show it better?"
  3. "What one question would extend or improve my reflective notes on this activity?"
